Zhongze Li

PhD Student · Department of Electrical Engineering · Tsinghua University

Reliable and scalable stability analysis for converter-dominated power systems across models, devices, and time scales.

My research focuses on two closely connected themes: understanding when reduced-order models remain trustworthy across electromagnetic and electromechanical time scales, and developing compositional or distributed stability certificates that infer system-level behavior from local device and network information.

Research

The common goal is to make stability analysis both trustworthy and scalable as converter models become more detailed, heterogeneous, and increasingly difficult to expose internally.

01 · MODEL FIDELITY

Multi-timescale Modeling & Robust Stability

When can an electromechanical or phasor-domain device model be trusted even though faster EMT dynamics are omitted? I study model mismatch as structured uncertainty and seek certificates that quantify when reduced-model stability conclusions remain valid.

EMT–phasor interaction model validity robust stability structured μ frequency response measurement-based uncertainty
02 · LOCAL TO GLOBAL

Compositional & Distributed Stability

How can large heterogeneous power systems be certified without assembling a fully centralized model? I study local-to-global conditions based on passivity, dissipativity, structured robustness, and network decomposition, with an emphasis on interpretable device-level margins.

passivity / dissipativity distributed criteria stability margins grid codes heterogeneous devices scalable analysis

Current Questions

A compact view of the research agenda behind the papers.

When is a reduced-order converter model sufficiently accurate for stability assessment?
How should unmodeled EMT dynamics be represented, identified, and robustly certified?
Can system-level stability margins be inferred from local device and network information?
How can distributed certificates remain useful when high-fidelity converter dynamics violate classical passivity assumptions?
How far can terminal frequency-response measurements replace unavailable internal models?
